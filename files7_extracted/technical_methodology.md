## 3. Match classes and notation
Four classes, always reported separately: **IPL league**, **IPL knockout** (Qualifier, Eliminator, Final), **T20I non-knockout**, **T20I final**. Indicators: A_t = 1 on IPL knockout deliveries; F_t = 1 on T20I final deliveries. "League" means IPL league only; the combined IPL league + T20I non-knockout output is called **regular-match** (D43). Delivery t, batter i or bowler j, opposing player o(t), match m(t); state s_t = (legal balls left, wickets lost, runs scored/required, innings, competition); x_t = PI_t - 0.5.

## 4. Stage 1: expected value (K3; D42)
E_t = expected runs and expected dismissal/wicket probability for a typical player given state, venue par, competition, and batter hand x bowler type (pace/spin + arm). **Stage 1 contains no player or team identity terms**, so the residual later used to estimate a player is never reduced by that player's own effect. Fitted stage-blind (no knockout or final indicator), cross-fitted by match; sensitivity: cross-fitting by player groups. Opposition quality is **not** a stage-1 term: it enters stage 2 as a crossed random effect estimated jointly (section 6). Ball value (K2): batter v = (r - E[r]) - c(s)(W - E[W]); bowler v = -((charged runs - E[charged runs])) + c(s)(wicket credited - E[wicket credited]); c(s) = expected remaining-runs loss from one wicket in state s (resource table). Wicket credit for bowlers: Bowled, Caught, LBW, Stump, Hit Wicket [KNOW]; batter dismissal = `dismissed_player_id`.

## 5. Win probability, leverage, pressure (K4-K6)
WP: interpretable logistic regression with natural splines, one per innings, joint model with a competition term, **stage-blind**, trained on the 771 WP-training matches and cross-fitted by match (venue par leave-one-match-out). Innings 1 key feature: projected margin = runs scored + r(balls left, wickets lost) - venue par; innings 2: r(balls left, wickets lost) - runs required, scaled by its spread. **Pressure is calculated for all pressure-eligible deliveries, including those in matches not used for training** (no-result, tie, super-over matches), from the cross-fitted WP of the state.
Leverage L(s) = sum_o P(o|s) |WP(s_o) - WP(s)|, o in {0, 1, 2-3, 4, 6, wicket} on legal balls; P(o|s) from a player-neutral multinomial logit (state and competition only). Diagnostic: sum_o P(o|s)(WP(s_o) - WP(s)) is about 0. PI_t = within-competition percentile of L; x_t = PI_t - 0.5; high-pressure reference PI* = 0.90, x* = 0.40.
**Worked numeric check (illustrative, K5):** state with WP = 45%. Outcome probabilities and WP after: dot 0.30 -> 33; single 0.30 -> 42; 2 or 3 runs 0.10 -> 52; four 0.12 -> 70; six 0.08 -> 88; wicket 0.10 -> 15 (probabilities sum to 1.00). Absolute swings 12, 3, 7, 25, 43, 30. L = 0.30x12 + 0.30x3 + 0.10x7 + 0.12x25 + 0.08x43 + 0.10x30 = 3.60 + 0.90 + 0.70 + 3.00 + 3.44 + 3.00 = **14.64** points. Coherence: expected WP after = 9.9 + 12.6 + 5.2 + 8.4 + 7.04 + 1.5 = 44.64 (about 45). Easy chase, WP = 97%, same probabilities, WP after 96.0, 97.2, 97.8, 98.8, 99.3, 92.0: swings 1.0, 0.2, 0.8, 1.8, 2.3, 5.0; L = 0.30 + 0.06 + 0.08 + 0.216 + 0.184 + 0.50 = **1.34** points (expected WP after 96.74).
**K2 numeric check (illustrative):** over 18, 5 down; E[r] = 1.5 runs; E[W] = **1/12 (= 0.0833)**; c = 6 runs. Six: (6 - 1.5) - 6(0 - 1/12) = 4.5 + 0.5 = +5.0. Dot: -1.5 + 0.5 = -1.0. Out for 0: -1.5 - 6(1 - 1/12) = -1.5 - 5.5 = -7.0.

### 5.1 Illegal deliveries, free hits and pressure (K19, D47)
Step 1 stores for every delivery the **pre-delivery state** s_t = (legal balls bowled so far, wickets lost, runs so far, innings, target in innings 2) and `delivery_kind` in {legal, wide, overstep, other}. Rules:
1. **Win-probability training rows are the pre-delivery states of legal deliveries only.** The pre-states of wides and no-balls repeat a balls-left state that the following legal ball also occupies and would double count it.
2. **Leverage L(s) is defined for a state from which a legal delivery is bowled.** Its outcome set {0, 1, 2-3, 4, 6, wicket} and the probabilities P(o|s) come from legal deliveries only. A wide or a no-ball is never treated as a draw from that distribution and never enters the sum, so legal-ball transitions are not applied to illegal deliveries.
3. **Free hits are modelled separately.** A free hit is the first non-wide delivery after a no-ball-like delivery (`overstep` or `other`); a wide in between does not use it up. Step 1 flags 918 deliveries (873 pressure-eligible). [DATA] Their wicket rate is 0.44% (4 dismissals, all run-outs) against 5.44% on other deliveries, their boundary rate is 33.6% against 17.9%, and none has a bowler-credited dismissal. Free-hit states get their own outcome model (wickets by run-out only, higher boundary probabilities) for leverage and expected value. The same pattern after `other` supports treating it as no-ball-like (A1).
4. **An illegal delivery (wide, no-ball, other) is assigned the pressure of the state it was bowled from:** PI_t = PI(s_t), the pressure of the moment. After it the runs advance and the legal-ball count does not; the next delivery has its own pre-state. This is a definition of exposure, not an outcome calculation.
5. **Stage-1 expected value for bowlers is defined per delivery bowled** with a delivery-type mixture: P(type | s) over {legal, wide, no-ball-like}, then type-specific outcomes (wide: 1-5 runs charged, rare stumping or run-out; no-ball-like: 1 penalty run plus bat runs 0-6, run-out only; legal: as in K2/K5). For batters a wide is not a ball faced and carries no batter value and no batter pressure exposure.
6. Pressure-eligible counts [DATA]: wides 7,244; no-ball-like 801; free-hit deliveries 873.
Sensitivity: S15 gives illegal deliveries the pressure of the next legal ball's state; S16 drops illegal deliveries from pressure-weighted performance. Checks: Step 1 B14 and B15 (no credited dismissal on a free hit; first delivery never a free hit); Step 3 will compare the leverage distribution of illegal and legal deliveries.

## 6. Player model: joint hierarchical model (K7-K14; D25-D29, D37, D42)
Separate model for batters and for bowlers, each a **crossed hierarchical model** on stage-1 residuals y_t (v_t):
 batters: y = mu_i + theta_i x + A(kappa + gamma x + delta_i) + F(nu + nu_g x) - beta_o(t) + m_m(t) + e
 bowlers: the same with bowler j, opposing batter effect alpha_o(t) and bowler value.
Priors: (mu_i, theta_i) ~ N((m_role, s_role), Sigma_role) with role = batting-order band (openers 1-2, top order 3-4, middle 5-6, lower 7+) or pace/spin; delta_i ~ N(0, psi^2) (pooled; role-specific psi^2 as sensitivity); beta_o ~ N(0, sigma_beta^2) crossed (**opposition quality estimated jointly in the same fit**, so no player's effect is first estimated from, then removed from, the data used to rate that player); m_m ~ N(0, sigma_m^2) match effect (handles within-match clustering); e ~ N(0, sigma^2). kappa, gamma: population IPL-knockout shift and knockout x pressure interaction; nu, nu_g: nuisance population terms for the five T20I finals (weak prior) so that the finals neither contaminate the regular-match estimate nor inform kappa, gamma or psi; the finals still inform the player's general stage-blind parameters mu_i, theta_i after their own population adjustment (D26).
**Outputs, at x\* = 0.40 (runs per 100 deliveries = 100 x value):**
 R^reg_i = mu_i + theta_i x\*  (regular-match: IPL league + T20I non-knockout)
 R^KO_i = R^reg_i + kappa + gamma x\* + delta_i  (IPL knockout)
 R_i = (1 - w_K) R^reg_i + w_K R^KO_i = R^reg_i + w_K (kappa + gamma x\* + delta_i)  (overall)
Supporting, descriptive, shown separately (not in R): IPL league, T20I non-knockout and T20I finals adjusted means with intervals; per-class deviations (shrunk to R^reg) only where the player has enough exposure.
**Reference weight (K13, D29).** w_K = sum of leverage L over eligible IPL knockout deliveries / sum of L over all eligible IPL deliveries. Calculated once, after the pressure model is validated and before any player-level result is viewed; the value, code version and time are recorded in the decision log and not changed afterwards. Distinction: *match frequency* = share of matches that are knockouts (20/371 = 5.39% of all IPL matches; 5.54% of pressure-eligible IPL matches); *delivery frequency* = share of deliveries (5.31% of pressure-eligible IPL deliveries; 5.05% of overs 16-20; 4.83% of innings-2 overs 16-20); *pressure exposure* = share of aggregate leverage, which counts a delivery in proportion to how much it can change the result, so it can differ from both. Only the first two proxies can be computed today [DATA]; the leverage share is expected to be close to them but is not known. Sensitivity: share of high-pressure deliveries (PI >= 0.8 and top quintile), delivery share, match share, and w_K = 10%, 20%.
**Shrinkage and estimation (K11).** The knockout estimate shrinks to the player's own regular-match estimate plus the population shift (delta_i shrinks to 0). This is *implemented through the full hierarchical model*, not the single-player formula B = psi^2/(psi^2 + sigma^2/n), which is kept only as an intuition aid. Estimation: REML for the mixed model using sparse mixed-model equations, giving the joint conditional covariance of all fixed effects and player effects; draws of (mu_i, theta_i, delta_i, kappa, gamma, ...) are taken jointly so R^reg, R^KO and R are computed draw by draw and carry the uncertainty of the league estimate, the population shift, the match clustering and the variance components (variance-component uncertainty from the REML curvature, and from the cluster bootstrap below). Final intervals and ranks: match-cluster bootstrap (resample matches, refit stage 2; stage 1 refitted on a subset of resamples). Pre-specified cross-check: a Bayesian fit of the same model; disagreement beyond the stated tolerance triggers review before any ranking is used.
**Reliability (K12).** rel = 1 - PostVar/PriorVar for R^reg, delta and R^KO. [DATA, unadjusted upper bound, runs only, IPL] role-specific knockout reliability at the median knockout sample (players with any knockout balls): all batters 0.15 (17.5 balls); openers 0.42 (42 balls); top order 0.10 (37.5); middle 0.18 (31.5); lower order 0.09 (5.0); all bowlers 0.14 (44 balls); pace 0.13 (46); spin 0.08 (27.5). Knockout balls needed for reliability 0.5: batters about 97 (openers 57, middle 149, top order 331, lower 49 from only 16 players); bowlers about 270 (pace 313, spin 317).
**Population preliminary estimates [DATA, unadjusted, IPL; players with >=120 league and >=12 knockout balls; units D32]:** kappa_hat batters -0.052 runs/ball (95% CI -0.134 to 0.032, 64 players), bowlers -0.029 (-0.108 to 0.050, 76 players); psi_hat batters 0.151 (0 to 0.248), bowlers 0.120 (0 to 0.219); 12.5% / 24% of bootstrap draws give psi = 0; weight on a player's own knockout deviation at the median 46.5 balls: 0.25 / 0.18. The adjusted joint model is expected to give smaller psi.
**Eligibility and pressure-exposure thresholds, role-aware (K15, D33, D49).** Roles follow a rule frozen in Step 1: batters by the modal batting order on their eligible balls (openers 1-2: 139 players; top order 3-4: 152; middle 5-6: 131; lower 7+: 394), bowlers by `pace_or_spin` (pace 341, spin 248). Thresholds come from a **pre-ranking power analysis (Step 5a)** that uses only population variance components from the fitted model (tau, sigma, psi by role), never an individual player's estimate or rank. For each role it finds the smallest eligible-delivery count n_min and the smallest pressure exposure h_min (deliveries with PI >= 0.8, and the effective information on the pressure response, the sum of squared centred PI) such that the posterior reliability of R^reg for a typical player of that role is at least 0.5 [ASSUME] and its standard error is at most half the between-player SD; a simulation confirms how often a true top-decile player of that role would enter an estimated top 10. The thresholds, the role rule, the code hash and the time are recorded in the decision log and **frozen before any player-level rating or ranking is inspected**; players below them are listed as not eligible with their counts, never ranked. **No knockout-exposure requirement.** Today 816 batters and 589 bowlers have at least one eligible delivery.
**6.3 Transfer diagnostic: does skill transfer across competitions? (D48, A5).** Feasibility [DATA, Step 1]: players with at least N eligible balls in both IPL and T20I: batters 87 / 68 / 37 and bowlers 97 / 79 / 38 for N = 60 / 120 / 300 (182 batters have any eligible ball in both). Design: (a) fit the player model with competition-specific baselines (mu^IPL_i, mu^T20I_i) and pressure responses with correlation rho, by role; (b) predictive transfer: fit on IPL only and predict the T20I residual means of dual-competition players, and the reverse, comparing the correlation with the within-competition split-half reliability ceiling (disattenuated transfer correlation = r_cross / sqrt(rel_IPL x rel_T20I)); (c) the sensitivity scenario S7 (IPL-only vs IPL + T20I top 10). Pre-specified rule [ASSUME]: shared skill stays primary if the 90% interval of the disattenuated correlation includes 0.8 or higher; otherwise an IPL-only ranking and a pooled ranking are both reported, the limitation is stated, and the choice of primary goes to you at the Step 5/6 gate using variance components only, before rankings are inspected. Caveat: players active in both competitions are a selected group (internationals who also play the IPL).
**6.4 What the intervals contain and the evidence required (D45).** REML returns the variance components and the *conditional* covariance of the player effects given those components. That interval omits the uncertainty of the variance components and any dependence the model does not describe. Three layers are added: (i) REML-curvature draws of the variance components; (ii) a match-cluster bootstrap *stratified by class* (IPL league, IPL knockout, T20I non-knockout, T20I final) so each resample keeps the number of knockout matches, refitting everything including the variance components; (iii) the Bayesian fit with priors on all variance components. **Demonstration before any interval is used (Step 5 gate):** a simulation drawn from the fitted population parameters with known true player effects, 100 replicates (bootstrap B = 200 per replicate; fewer replicates for the Bayesian fit), in two worlds: a correctly specified one and a stressed one with heavier-tailed errors and extra player-match dependence. For each interval method we record the empirical coverage of the nominal 90% interval and the width for R^reg, delta, R^KO and R. Acceptance [ASSUME]: coverage 85-95% for R^KO and R in the correct world and at least 80% in the stressed world for the bootstrap and Bayesian methods; the conditional-only interval is expected to undercover and is shown only as a lower bound. On the real data: the Bayesian / bootstrap interval-width ratio must lie in 0.85-1.15 and the rank correlation of posterior means must be at least 0.98, otherwise review before ranking. With only 20 IPL knockout matches the bootstrap is coarse, so the Bayesian check is mandatory.

## 7. League-match importance (K16; D31)
Fields: competition, season, `stage` (all IPL league matches = "League"), `match_number`, `match_date`, `win_team_id`, `result`. No points table, qualification rule, tie-break rule or remaining-fixture information. IPL 2022-24 and 2026: 10 teams x 14 league matches; IPL 2025: 71 league matches, one team on 15, dates not monotonic in `match_number` [D36]. **League-match tournament importance is not measurable and no importance score is built;** league matches are not claimed to be equal in stakes, only not distinguished by stakes. In-game leverage is the only importance measure used for league deliveries.

## 8. Model comparison and tests (D30, D34)
M0 stage-blind (no A, F terms; fitted on the same data), M1 separate fits by class, M2 the model in section 6. Criteria: match-grouped cross-validated predictive log-likelihood (leave-one-knockout-match-out for knockout deliveries, 19 folds with IPL 2023 Final innings 1 as a separate fold), Wald tests with match-clustered SEs for kappa, gamma, a boundary likelihood-ratio test for psi^2. **Equivalence:** a two one-sided test (90% CI inside the margin) at **three margins: 0.01 (primary-strict), 0.025 (primary), 0.05 (lenient) runs per delivery** (1.2, 3 and 6 runs per 120 deliveries). Outcome classes: *equivalent*, *different*, or **inconclusive** (interval too wide). A non-significant test is never reported as "no difference". [DATA, earlier base] unadjusted IPL league-vs-knockout runs per legal ball -0.005 (95% CI -0.099 to 0.089): wider than 0.05, so inconclusive at every margin so far.
**Decision rule (frozen):** M2 is the reporting model (the only one yielding three coherent outputs). M2 replaces M0 as primary unless it loses clearly on cross-validation, in which case M0 is primary and the reason is documented. No weight, margin or threshold is changed after rankings are viewed.
