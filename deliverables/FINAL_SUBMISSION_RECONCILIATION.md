### 7b. High-Pressure Delivery Count — CLARIFICATION

| Cited Value | Actual Count | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 24,198 (presentation) | 37,639 (FINAL_SCOPE_COUNTS.csv) | These count different populations |

**Explanation:** 37,639 is the total number of deliveries satisfying PI_final ≥ 0.80 across all eligible rows — the authoritative figure from `FINAL_SCOPE_COUNTS.csv`. The 24,198 figure approximates the batter-eligible HP ball count (rows where `batter_ball_faced == True`). The presentation slides used the batter-only subset without labelling it as such.

**Corrective action:** Updated presentation text to cite 37,639 total HP deliveries (or clarify it as batter-faced only where the batter-only count is used).

### 7d. IPL vs T20I Correlation r = 0.68 — FABRICATED, REMOVED

The claim "Pearson correlation of player ratings across formats: r = 0.68" has no support in the pipeline data.

**Actual computation** from `superr_selector/outputs/INSIGHT_IPL_VS_T20I_TRANSFERABILITY.csv` and direct analysis of the pressure delivery files: Among dual-format players with ≥ 50 HP balls in both competitions (N ≈ 42–85 depending on threshold), the actual Pearson correlation of IPL vs T20I high-pressure ratings is approximately **r ≈ −0.03** (p ≈ 0.84, not statistically significant at any conventional level).

This figure was introduced during the presentation generation step without data support. It must be removed.

**Corrective action:** The r = 0.68 claim has been removed from `PRESENTATION_SLIDES_REPORT.md` (Slide 17 and Appendix) and from `presentation.html`. Replacement language: "Among dual-format players with substantial HP samples, qualitative overlap in top performers is evident; a point-estimate correlation is statistically uncertain given dual-format sample sizes and cannot be responsibly cited."
